Most families who come to our office in Airport Housing Society end up choosing between two sizes: **5 marla** and **10 marla**. Both are common in societies across Rawalpindi and Islamabad, both are easy to find for sale and rent, and both can make a comfortable home. The real question is not which size is "better" but which one fits your family, your budget and your plans for the next ten years. Here is how we explain the difference to buyers.

## First, how big is each size?

In most modern housing societies around Rawalpindi and Islamabad, one marla is counted as **225 sq ft**. On that basis:

| | 5 marla | 10 marla |
|---|---|---|
| Land area (225 sq ft marla) | 1,125 sq ft | 2,250 sq ft |
| Usual build | Double storey on a compact footprint | Double storey with wider rooms and more open space |
| Parking | Usually space for one car | Often space for two cars |
| Best suited to | Small families, first-time buyers | Larger and joint families, long-term owners |

The marla is not the same everywhere. Older land records and some areas still use **272.25 sq ft**, and plot dimensions (for example 25 × 45 ft) vary between societies. Check the society map, and use our [marla calculator](/tools/marla-calculator/) or our [marla, kanal and square feet guide](/guides/marla-kanal-square-feet-guide/) to convert sizes before you compare prices.

## Layout and rooms

The number of rooms you get depends on the design and the society's bylaws (setbacks, covered area limits and height), so treat the layouts below as typical patterns rather than fixed rules.

### A typical 5 marla house

To get enough rooms on 5 marla, houses are almost always built double storey. A common arrangement is:

- **Ground floor:** drawing room or lounge, kitchen, one or two bedrooms with attached baths, and a small car porch
- **First floor:** two bedrooms with baths, a TV lounge and sometimes a second kitchen
- **Roof:** staircase room (mumty) and sometimes a store or small extra room

The trade-offs are smaller rooms, a narrow staircase, limited parking and less natural light in the middle of the house. Good design, such as a skylight or careful window placement, helps a lot.

### A typical 10 marla house

The wider frontage and extra depth of a 10 marla plot change how the house feels. You can usually fit:

- A separate drawing room and dining area
- Larger bedrooms, each with its own bath and dressing area
- A front lawn or open space, and a porch for two cars
- A servant room or laundry area
- Floors designed as **self-contained portions** with separate entrances and kitchens

That last point matters. Many families build a 10 marla house so that parents and a married son can live on separate floors, or so that one floor can be rented out.

## Running and maintenance costs

A bigger house costs more to live in, even when it is paid for. Before you stretch your budget, think about:

- **Electricity and gas:** more rooms usually means more air conditioners, heaters and lights.
- **Upkeep:** repainting, roof waterproofing, plumbing repairs and termite treatment all cost more on a larger area.
- **Society charges:** in many societies, maintenance and conservancy charges are linked to plot size. Ask the society office for the current schedule.
- **Property tax:** this depends on size and location. Confirm the current amount with the Excise and Taxation department rather than relying on what the seller paid years ago.

## Rental demand

In our experience, **5 marla houses and portions** are in steady demand because their rent is within reach of more households, including small families and working couples. Vacancy periods tend to be shorter, but tenants also move more often.

**10 marla houses** attract a smaller pool of tenants: larger families, and sometimes offices or organisations where society rules allow it. These tenants often stay longer. A 10 marla house built as two portions gives you a choice: rent the whole house to one family, or rent each floor separately.

In both cases, the sector, street width and distance to schools, markets and main roads affect rent more than the plot size alone. For current rents in a specific area, ask a local agent instead of relying on online averages.

## Resale

- **5 marla** has a wider buyer pool because more families can afford it, so a well-located 5 marla house is usually easier to sell.
- **10 marla** has fewer buyers at that budget, but they tend to be serious end-users. A well-built 10 marla house in a good sector can sell well, but it may take longer to find the right buyer.

For either size, the things that help resale most are clean documents, an approved map that matches the construction, good build quality, and a corner, park-facing or wide-street location.

## Budget: look beyond the asking price

A 10 marla house in a sector is not simply double the price of a 5 marla house in the same sector. Per-marla rates differ by size, location, construction age and quality. Compare houses **per marla of land and per square foot of covered area**, not just by total price.

Also budget for transfer costs and taxes, any repairs or renovation, and furnishing a larger space. A common trade-off is choosing between a 5 marla house in an established area such as [Airport Housing Society](/areas/airport-housing-society/) and a 10 marla house in a newer, less developed society for a similar budget. Location usually wins over size when it comes to daily life and future value.

## Which one should you choose?

**A 5 marla house is usually the better choice if:**

- You are a small family or first-time buyer
- You want lower bills and easier maintenance
- You may sell or rent the house in a few years

**A 10 marla house is usually the better choice if:**

- You live as a joint family or expect your family to grow
- You need parking for two cars or want a lawn
- You plan to stay long term, or want to rent out one floor
