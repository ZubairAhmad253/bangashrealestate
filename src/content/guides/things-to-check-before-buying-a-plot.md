A plot looks simple: an empty piece of land with a number on it. In practice, plots carry more hidden risk than built houses, because there is nothing on the ground to show you whether the society is legal, the land is developed or the seller has the right to sell. In Rawalpindi and Islamabad, most of the problems we are asked to sort out come from buyers who paid a token on a plot before checking the basics. Use this checklist before you commit.

## 1. Is the society approved?

Start with the society, not the plot. Find out which authority it falls under: the **Rawalpindi Development Authority (RDA)** for most societies in the Rawalpindi area, the **Capital Development Authority (CDA)** in Islamabad, or a **cantonment board** in cantonment areas.

- Check the authority's latest published lists of approved and unapproved schemes, or visit its office.
- Confirm that the **specific phase, block or extension** you are buying in is part of the approved layout plan. An approval for one part of a scheme does not automatically cover new extensions.
- Don't rely on brochures, billboards or a sales office telling you the society is "approved". Ask to see the approval and verify it yourself.

## 2. Ownership and title

- The seller's name must match their **CNIC** and the society's records.
- Ask for the **allotment letter, file and transfer history**, and confirm them at the society office.
- If the seller is acting under a **power of attorney**, verify the document and confirm with the society that it is accepted.
- If the plot was inherited, all legal heirs may need to be part of the sale, usually through a succession certificate or similar document.
- For land outside housing societies, check the **fard**, **registry** and **intiqal (mutation)** in the revenue record.

Our [property documents checklist](/guides/property-documents-checklist-rawalpindi-islamabad/) covers these papers in more detail.

## 3. File or possession plot?

This is one of the most important questions in the Rawalpindi and Islamabad market.

| | File | Possession plot |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | A right to a plot, sometimes not yet allocated | A specific, demarcated plot handed over to the owner |
| Can you build? | No, not until possession | Yes, after map approval |
| Main risk | Delays, or the land not being available or developed | Lower, if documents and dues are clear |
| Price | Usually lower | Usually higher |

Files are traded actively, and some buyers profit from them, but you are buying a promise. Ask whether the plot has been **balloted** (allocated a number), whether the land in that block is actually owned and developed by the society, and what the society has said about possession.

## 4. Dues and charges

Unpaid dues stay with the plot, not the seller. Ask the society office for a written statement of:

- Outstanding installments
- **Development charges**
- Maintenance or conservancy charges
- Transfer fees and any other charges payable at transfer
- Any charges for extra land, corner or park-facing location

Some societies also apply charges when a plot is left unbuilt for a long period. Ask whether this applies to the plot you are considering.

## 5. Demarcation and measurements

- Visit the plot with the society's map and confirm that the number on the ground matches the paperwork.
- Check the boundary pillars and measure the plot yourself. Compare the dimensions with the map.
- Confirm whether the society uses a **225 sq ft** or **272.25 sq ft** marla. Our [marla calculator](/tools/marla-calculator/) makes this easy.
- If the plot is larger than its file size, ask whether the society will charge for the extra land.

## 6. Location, access and level

Two plots with the same size and file can have very different value. Check:

- **Access road:** its width, whether it is carpeted, and how directly it connects to the main road
- **Level:** plots well below road level need filling, and sloping land, which is common in parts of Rawalpindi, can need extra foundation work
- **Surroundings:** nullahs, high-tension wires, graveyards, open drains or busy commercial areas nearby
- **Category:** corner, park-facing and boulevard plots cost more, so make sure the plot really is what it is sold as

## 7. Utilities and development

Ask whether **electricity, gas, water and sewerage** are available in that block or only planned. Look at the street: are there poles, pipelines and houses already built and occupied? Talking to residents nearby often tells you more than the sales office.

## 8. Building rules

Every society has bylaws on setbacks, covered area, number of floors and permitted use. A plot marked residential cannot be used for a shop or office. Read the rules before you buy, especially if you are planning flats or portions. Our guide on [house construction cost in Rawalpindi](/guides/house-construction-cost-rawalpindi/) explains what to budget for once you start building.

## 9. Paying safely

- Pay a token only after the checks above, and always against a written **agreement to sell** with the price, schedule and transfer date.
- Pay through traceable methods such as a pay order or crossed cheque in the owner's name rather than cash.
- Complete the transfer at the society office with both parties present.
