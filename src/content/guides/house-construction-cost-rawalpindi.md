"How much will it cost to build my house?" is the first question almost every plot owner asks us. The honest answer is that it depends on your design, your finishing choices and the price of materials in the month you build. Cement, steel and labour rates in Rawalpindi move often, so we don't publish a fixed per-square-foot figure here; it would be out of date within weeks. What we can do is explain what goes into the price, so you can plan a realistic budget and compare quotes with confidence.

## How builders usually quote

Before comparing numbers, make sure you know what kind of quote you are looking at:

- **Per square foot of covered area:** the most common method. Covered area is the built area of all floors, not the plot size. A double-storey house on a 5 marla plot has far more covered area than 1,125 sq ft.
- **Grey structure rate or complete rate:** a grey structure rate covers only the shell, while a complete (turnkey) rate includes finishing. See our guide on [grey structure vs turnkey construction](/guides/grey-structure-vs-turnkey-construction/).
- **With material or labour only:** a labour-only rate looks much lower because you buy the materials yourself.
- **Lump sum with a BOQ:** a fixed total backed by a bill of quantities listing every item.

Two quotes are only comparable if they use the same method and the same specification.

## The main cost components

### Design and approvals

Architectural plans, structural design and elevation drawings, followed by map approval from your society or the relevant authority (RDA, CDA or the cantonment board, depending on location). Approval fees are set by that office and differ between societies, so get the current schedule directly from them.

### Grey structure

Excavation, foundation, steel, cement, sand, crush, bricks, shuttering and labour. This is where the strength of your house is decided, and it is not the place to cut corners.

### Finishing

Usually the most variable part of the budget. Tiles or marble, doors and windows, woodwork, kitchen, bathrooms, electrical wiring and fittings, paint, ceilings and railings can be done at a basic, standard or premium level, and the difference between these levels is large.

### External works and services

Boundary wall and gate, underground and overhead water tanks, sewer or septic connection, a water bore where the area needs one, ramps, and any landscaping.

## What pushes the cost up or down

- **Covered area and floors:** more floors and rooms mean more material and labour.
- **Basement:** adds excavation, retaining walls and waterproofing, and can raise the budget considerably.
- **Design complexity:** cantilevers, large column-free spaces, double-height lounges and heavy elevation features all need more steel and skilled work.
- **Soil and site conditions:** plots below road level, filled ground or sloping land, which you see in parts of Rawalpindi, may need extra filling or a deeper foundation.
- **Finishing level:** imported fittings and premium tiles can change the total more than any other single choice.
- **Material prices:** cement, steel and bricks fluctuate, and imported items move with the exchange rate.
- **Site access:** narrow streets make material delivery and handling harder.
- **Changes midway:** moving walls or changing the design after work has started is one of the most common reasons budgets overrun.

## How to get and compare quotes

1. Give every builder the **same drawings and the same specification**.
2. Ask for an **itemised BOQ**, not just a single rate.
3. Check the details: steel grade, cement brand, brick quality, concrete mix, slab thickness, and tile and fitting ranges.
4. Read the **exclusions** carefully. A low quote often leaves out plaster, water tanks, the boundary wall or electrical fittings.
5. Ask how long the rate is valid, and what happens if material prices rise during the project.
6. Visit the builder's **ongoing sites**, not only finished houses, and speak to past clients if you can.
7. Be careful with a quote far below the others. The difference usually reappears later as extra charges or lower-quality material.

## How stage payments typically work

Most construction contracts in Rawalpindi and Islamabad are paid in stages linked to work completed. A typical sequence looks like this:

| Stage | Payment is usually linked to |
|---|---|
| Start | Signing the agreement and mobilising the team |
| Foundation | Excavation, foundation and plinth completed |
| Structure | Each roof slab cast |
| Masonry | Brickwork and plaster completed |
| Finishing | Major finishing milestones (flooring, woodwork, electrical, paint) |
| Handover | Final inspection and snag list resolved |

Stage names and amounts are agreed in the contract. The principle is simple: payments should follow progress, not run far ahead of it. Keep receipts for every payment and ask for regular photo updates if you can't visit the site.

## Hidden costs owners often forget

- Map approval, revised map approval if the design changes, and completion certificate fees
- Electricity, gas and water connection charges, meters and demand notices (IESCO for electricity and SNGPL for gas; the availability of new gas connections depends on current policy, so check before you plan around it)
- Water for construction, by tanker or bore, if it isn't in the contract
- Any construction deposit or charges your society may take before work starts
- Debris removal and site cleaning
- Items outside the contract: air conditioners, light fixtures, geysers, solar panels, wardrobes and curtains
- Rent for your current home while construction is going on
- A contingency amount for price increases and small changes
